## Pick a weapon for the job
Your gun solves an immediate problem, but every shot can pull more zombies into the area. Keep a quiet melee weapon ready for isolated enemies and save firearm ammunition for a dangerous group, a teammate who is about to be surrounded, or a target you cannot safely route around. A firearm is strongest when the team has room to retreat after firing.

The current known roster includes sidearms such as the M9A3 and M1911, compact weapons such as the MP5, rifles including the M14 and M7A1, and heavier choices including the Rochester 1873, Hunter 85, MC-15, and 590A1. Use the tier list for role-by-role recommendations rather than assuming every weapon should be played the same way.

## Make every magazine count
Do not carry ammunition inside a gun you no longer plan to use. Hold **U** to unload it and recover the rounds before you switch weapons, share ammunition, or make room in your bag. This is especially useful when you find a better gun with a different caliber: unload first, then decide whether the old weapon is worth keeping.

Avoid turning a small encounter into a long firefight. If the squad can run around the group or use melee without being boxed in, do that. Loud, repeated shots are more costly than a single clean rescue shot because they create more pressure after the first zombies fall.

## Use melee when you have space
Melee weapons fall into **Blunt** and **Edged** types, and charged attacks give you a stronger hit when you have enough room and stamina to commit. Use charged attacks against a separated target, not when several zombies can punish the recovery animation.

- **Blunt weapons** are a good choice when you need space and stagger control.
- **Edged weapons** are useful when you want to remove limbs and reduce a zombie's ability to reach you.
- Back away after each committed swing. If your route is blocked or stamina is low, switch plans before the next attack instead of forcing another hit.

## Fit attachments before the fight
Optics, suppressors, rail attachments, and flashlights can change how a weapon feels, but an attachment only helps if it matches the weapon. Check compatibility before relying on a pickup.

- Use an **optic** when you need clearer target placement at range.
- Use a **flashlight** for dark interiors, then turn it off when you no longer need it to conserve batteries.
- Use a **suppressor** to reduce how far gunfire draws zombies. It does not make shots silent, so keep an escape route and do not fire carelessly near a large group.

## Build a safe loadout
For a solo run, favor a weapon you can feed consistently and a melee backup that keeps enemies at arm's length. In co-op, split jobs: one player can use an accurate rifle for priority targets while another carries a close-range or crowd-control weapon. Tell teammates before you unload, swap ammunition, or start shooting so the team can cover the approach and the retreat.

Weapon balance can change with patches. Test unfamiliar gear in a low-pressure fight before trusting it during extraction.
